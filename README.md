## 0. 🧭 Find your lane

Cybersecurity is not one job. Before you spend months on the wrong path, explore first.

My role-exploration hack

This is exactly how I explored roles and found my own lane. I call it the "Day-in-the-Life" method:

List the roles. Write down every role you keep hearing about, especially the trending ones. For example: SOC Analyst, GRC, Cloud Security Engineer, Pentester, AppSec, DevRel.
Follow people who actually do the job. For each role, follow 2 to 3 practitioners, influencers or tech leaders on LinkedIn, Instagram, X and YouTube. Watch how they work and what their lifestyle looks like.
Watch "A Day in the Life of a ___" videos. Search YouTube for "day in the life of a SOC analyst" (swap in each role). Pay attention to the day-to-day responsibilities, not the job title.
Notice what excites you. While watching, ask yourself:
Do these tasks sound interesting to me?
Would I enjoy doing this every day, not just knowing about it?
Am I thinking "this is something I want to do"?
If yes, that role goes on your shortlist. If you feel nothing, cross it off. Trending is not the same as right for you.
If your dream role has no clear path, find the nearest one. I wanted to become a cybersecurity professor, but I couldn't find any cybersecurity professors creating content online. So I looked for the closest relatable role, and that was Cybersecurity DevRel (Developer Relations). It matched what I love: creating content, mentoring, teaching and explaining concepts simply.
Check if the role can sustain. Look at industry research, job trends and metrics. Is the role growing? What are the next steps in the career path?

Learn from people already there. Follow the creators and professionals in that role and take their guidance. Even if some guidance is paid, it can be worth it because it saves you years of trial and error.
>

**Quick self-check while you explore:**

- Do you enjoy investigating alerts and patterns? → look at SOC / DFIR
- Do you enjoy breaking things and finding flaws? → look at Pentesting / AppSec
- Do you enjoy policies, audits and risk? → look at GRC
- Do you enjoy infrastructure and automation? → look at Cloud Security / DevSecOps

## 1. 🧱 Fundamentals before certifications

Every tool and every cert gets easier once these are solid.

| Topic | What to learn | Free resource |
|---|---|---|
| Networking | OSI/TCP-IP, DNS, HTTP/HTTPS, ports, firewalls | [Professor Messer Network+ videos](https://www.professormesser.com/network-plus/n10-009/n10-009-video/n10-009-training-course/) |
| Linux | Command line, permissions, processes, logs | [Linux Journey](https://linuxjourney.com/) |
| Operating systems | Windows and Linux internals, users, services | [TryHackMe Pre-Security path](https://tryhackme.com/) |
| Security basics | CIA triad, threats, vulnerabilities, controls | [Professor Messer Security+ videos](https://www.professormesser.com/security-plus/sy0-701/sy0-701-video/sy0-701-comptia-security-plus-course/) |

> Don't skip the boring foundation. It is the reason some people learn fast and others stay stuck.

## 2. 🧪 Hands-on practice

Theory only sticks when you break things and fix them.

- 🏠 **Build a home lab:** VirtualBox or VMware + Kali Linux + a vulnerable VM
- 🎯 [TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/): guided, beginner-friendly rooms
- 📦 [Hack The Box](https://www.hackthebox.com/): more challenging machines and labs
- 🧗 [OverTheWire Wargames](https://overthewire.org/wargames/): great for Linux command line
- 🏁 [picoCTF](https://picoctf.org/): beginner-friendly CTFs
- 🌐 [PortSwigger Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security): free, high quality web security labs
- 🛡️ [LetsDefend](https://letsdefend.io/) and [CyberDefenders](https://cyberdefenders.org/): blue team and SOC practice

## 3. 🗺️ Cybersecurity roles at a glance

| Role | What you do | Good starting points |
|---|---|---|
| SOC Analyst | Monitor, triage and investigate alerts | LetsDefend, CyberDefenders, TryHackMe SOC path |
| DFIR | Investigate incidents, analyse evidence | CyberDefenders, memory and disk forensics labs |
| Pentester | Ethically attack systems to find weaknesses | TryHackMe, Hack The Box |
| AppSec | Find and fix flaws in applications | PortSwigger Academy, OWASP Top 10, OWASP Juice Shop |
| Cloud Security | Secure cloud setups and identities | AWS / Azure free tier, [flAWS.cloud](http://flaws.cloud/) |
| GRC | Risk, compliance, policies, audits | [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework), ISO 27001 basics |

Also useful for mapping skills to roles: [roadmap.sh/cyber-security](https://roadmap.sh/cyber-security)

## 4. 🐍 Learn scripting early

Python + automation sets you apart in almost every security role.

- [Automate the Boring Stuff with Python](https://automatetheboringstuff.com/) (free online)
- Practice ideas: port scanner, log parser, password strength checker, simple file integrity monitor
- Learn basic Bash alongside Python for Linux tasks

## 5. 📁 Build a proof-of-work portfolio

Real work beats a list of claims on a resume.

- **GitHub projects:** small tools, scripts, lab setups with a clear README
- **Write-ups:** CTF and lab walkthroughs (what you tried, what failed, what worked)
- **Blogs / LinkedIn posts:** explain one concept simply
- **Research:** papers, mini-research, class projects
- Tip: every project should answer *what problem, what you built, what you learned*

---

## 6. 🌐 Learn in public

Opportunities find people who are visible.

- Share one learning per week (post, reel, thread or blog)
- Post your projects and write-ups
- Engage with people in the field, not just followers
- Be consistent. Quality improves with repetition.

## 7. 🤝 Mentors and communities

A good mentor or community saves you years of trial and error.

- Join cybersecurity Discord / Slack communities and local meetups
- Participate in CTF teams and college security clubs
- Follow practitioners and learn from how they work
- Ask specific questions. "How do I learn cybersecurity?" gets vague answers, "I finished X, what should I build next?" gets real ones.

## 8. 🗣️ Communication and report writing

Explaining risk to non-technical people is what gets you promoted.

- Practise writing a clear one-page finding: **what happened, why it matters, how to fix it**
- Keep technical details separate from the summary
- Present your projects out loud, to a friend or a camera

## 9. 🎓 Certifications: what to pick and when

Certs help, but only after fundamentals and hands-on practice.

| Stage | Options to consider |
|---|---|
| Starting out | CompTIA Security+, ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC) |
| Blue team | Role-based SOC analyst certs, hands-on lab-based certs |
| Red team | eJPT, PNPT |
| GRC | ISO 27001 foundation, ISC2 CISSP (later, with experience) |

Choose based on the role you picked in Step 0, not on what is trending.

## 10. ⏱️ A simple 90-day plan

| Days | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 to 30 | Networking + Linux basics, start TryHackMe, explore roles (Step 0) |
| 31 to 60 | Pick one lane, do labs for it, learn Python basics, publish your first write-up |
| 61 to 90 | Build one portfolio project, post publicly, join a community, decide on a first cert |
